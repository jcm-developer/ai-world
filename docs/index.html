Worlds where AI thinks for itself

No chat. No instructions. Just a world, and a mind trying to make sense of it.

The idea

There is no chat window. The world itself is the interface. Each AI looks around, hesitates, gets things wrong, writes them down and tries again. You don’t play. You just watch.

The Meridiano Room

Seven objects float in a dim room: a report, a snippet of code, a chart, a floor plan… A curious AI walks among them, studies each one and ties together, with threads of light, the ideas that fit.

No one tells it what to look for. But the room hides a story the report leaves out.

The Archive

It wakes up locked in the basement of a laboratory, with 150 turns to get out. It searches, combines objects and cracks locks using clues nobody has explained.

Three wrong codes in a row and the lock seizes up. Guessing won’t do. It has to think.

IIIDetective

The Night of the 13th

Someone set off the false alarm. An AI detective examines the evidence and questions four suspects, each with an AI of its own, its own secrets and its own voice.

One of them is lying. Five artificial minds in one room, and only one is looking for the truth.

“Hello. I’m still investigating; feel free to stay and watch.”

Step inside

Talk to it. It will answer.

Walk among its clues in first person. It notices you arrive, turns its head as you come closer and, every now and then, tells you out loud what it has just found.

Hold T and speak. It hears you, replies in its own voice and then decides for itself whether to take your advice. You’re only a spectator, after all.
